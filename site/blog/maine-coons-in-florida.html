Can a Maine Coon handle Florida heat?

November 4, 2026

It's the question we hear most. Maine Coons were shaped by long, snowy New England winters, and we live a few minutes from the beach in Daytona. So how does that work?

The short answer: very well, as long as they live indoors and you help them with their coat.

Their coat adjusts

A Maine Coon's coat thickens and thins with the seasons. In a warm climate they carry a lighter coat for most of the year. Expect a bigger shed in spring, when they drop whatever extra fluff they grew over the winter.

Indoors, with air conditioning

Our cats live inside, where it's cool. They still get all the sunshine they want from the windows, and you can see from the photos that they take full advantage. Outdoor time in Florida brings heat, cars, wildlife and parasites, so we recommend keeping your Maine Coon indoors.

Brush often

A good brushing two or three times a week keeps the coat from matting and cuts down on shedding around the house. Pay extra attention to the belly, the back of the legs and behind the ears, where tangles start.

Water and shade

Keep fresh water in more than one spot. Many Maine Coons love running water, so a fountain is a good investment. And always leave a cool, shady place to retreat to, even indoors.

What about shaving?

Most Maine Coons don't need it, and their coat helps insulate them from heat as well as cold. If matting gets bad, talk with your veterinarian or a cat groomer before shaving.
